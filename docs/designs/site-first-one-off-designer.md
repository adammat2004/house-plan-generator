## Problem Statement

People in Ireland who will really build a one-off house go from "we have a site" to "we've hired someone" with little more than inspiration. Then a professional starts from a blank page. The house has to suit the family and also the county's rural design guide. The guide decides where the house sits on the site and what its exterior looks like: form, plan depth, roof, openings and materials. No tool lets someone design the house on its real site, inside those rules, and hand a professional something they can keep working on.

The app today has a strong plan engine (right-angle outlines, zone layouts ranked by plan sense, two storeys, stairs, hipped roofs, furniture, 3D). For the last few weeks it has been matching Drafted feature for feature, and it has no clear user.

## Demand Evidence

None yet. The founder is the only user, and no self-builder, builder or technician has seen the app. The founder's own words: "for myself in the future I want to build houses and design them myself rather than get an architect to design it."

Market context, which is not demand: Ireland completed 5,929 one-off houses in 2025, the most in the CSO series since 2011 ([thejournal.ie](https://www.thejournal.ie/housing-completions-apartments-2025-6941507-Jan2026/), [Selfbuild](https://selfbuild.ie/news/single-dwelling-completion-ireland)). Each one needs planning permission and a commencement notice under the building control regulations.

## Status Quo

This is a guess and has not been checked with anyone. Before hiring an architect, self-builders probably use plan books, Pinterest, Selfbuild magazine and shows, boards.ie and Facebook self-build groups, and their own sketches. The professional then starts again from scratch. Desktop tools for this audience are [Visual Building](https://visualbuilding.co.uk/), [HBXL PlansXpress](https://hbxl.co.uk/house-building/), SketchUp and Chief Architect. AI exterior tools such as ArchyBase and HomeGPT restyle a photo; they produce no buildable geometry. The Assignment checks the status quo with professionals.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **User:** a person in Ireland who will build a one-off house, plus the small "creative builder" the founder wants to become ("let creative builders be designers"). Ireland comes first; the UK follows with its own design codes.
- **Product:** "design your dream home", done on the real site, inside the county's rules, ending in a pack a professional refines.
- **Narrowest wedge:** site plus rule check for every council in Ireland (Phases 0–2; all of Ireland from the start, decided 2026-10-21). The founder chose the full product (Approach B) over the one-county wedge (Approach C). The phasing below puts the wedge first, so real people can try it before the rest is built. The wedge has no export. The hand off is tested by the Assignment, with professionals, before any of it is built (Phase 4).

## Constraints

- **Founder:** software-side only, with no self-builder, builder or technician among friends or family. Every rule and workflow assumption needs checking with someone outside.
- **Professionals are a choice, not a legal requirement.**
  - Irish law doesn't require a registered professional to prepare planning drawings.
  - Since [S.I. 365/2015](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2015/si/365/made/en/print), an owner building a single house for themselves can opt out of statutory certification. They still have to comply with the building regulations.
  - Handing off to a professional is therefore the founder's chosen positioning ("it shouldn't replace professionals"), not a legal gate.
  - Some self-builders will take the pack straight to the council. The pack must say on every page that it is not a set of planning drawings, and that it hasn't been checked for building regulations.
- **Rule values come from cited sources.** Every threshold is copied from a cited document, with its edition and page. Usually that is the county guide. Some checks rest on national documents instead: wastewater separation distances on the EPA Code of Practice for domestic waste water treatment systems, and entrance sightlines on the road design standards the county guide refers to. Nothing is invented.
- **What the code does today:**
  - `src/domain/project/site.ts` models a suburban rectangle: plot width and depth, setbacks and north.
  - Roofs are hipped only, at a fixed 35° (ADR 0014).
  - Exports are PNG, the project file and GLB. There is no scaled PDF, DXF or IFC.
- **Existing limits:** at most two WebGL contexts (viewer plus the shared stills renderer). AI keys stay on the server, and e2e tests blank them.

## Premises

1. **A dream-home designer for people who will really build.** Ireland first, because one-off houses are where the rules and the hand off matter most. Agreed after pushback: the founder rejected a narrow-niche framing and wants the broad "dream home" pitch, made distinct by its rules and its hand off.
2. **It does most of the professional's early work, then hands off.** The hand off is a first-class feature: files a professional can open (DXF/DWG, IFC later, scaled PDF) plus an owner brief. Agreed. (Corrected after review: professionals are legally optional for a self-built single house, so this is a positioning choice; see Constraints.)
3. **Site plus house, rules not styles** (revised after the second opinion). The house is designed on its real site, and both siting and exterior follow the county guide, generated from the same geometry as the plan. It is not a picker of AI-rendered styles. Agreed.
4. **Professionals decide whether the hand off is worth anything.** The test is a technician or architect saying "this would save me hours", checked before the export is built. Agreed.

## Recommended Approach: B, site-first one-off house designer

The founder chose this. Each phase ends with something a real person can try. Phases 0–2 together are the wedge: site plus rule check, for every council in Ireland.

The wireframe is at `~/.gstack/projects/Floor-plan-generator/designs/sketch-20261020/sketch.png` (`sketch.html` beside it). It shows three moments: Site (boundary on satellite, house placed and rotated, siting rules), Exterior (roof, pitch, form, walls and windows, each with what the guide says) and Hand off (the pack, plus "send to my architect / technician"). Two notes on the Site screen:
- It shows the site as it looks when you come back to it after picking a layout. On the first visit there is only the buildable area, as described in Phase 1.
- Its contour and skyline results are illustrative. Until there is terrain data they read "Measure on site".

**Phase 0: evidence, no code**
- Every council's rural house design guide (26 counties, 31 councils), not one county. Claude does the first extraction pass from each guide's PDF; the founder spot-checks a sample of each pack.
- Extract its rules into a table: rule, the source's wording, the source document with its edition and page, what to measure, thresholds.
- Tally the refusal reasons on at least 40 refused one-off house applications from that county's planning portal, decided in the last three years. Forty is enough for a majority call to be clear by more than about 15 points; the second opinion's 50 is better if the portal has them. The coding rule:
  - Leave out refusals whose only reasons are rural housing need or settlement policy. These are about who may build, not the site or the house.
  - **Site reasons:** position on the site, setbacks, access, traffic safety and sightlines, wastewater and percolation, and visual impact the refusal ties to the location (elevated, skyline, open landscape, ribbon development).
  - **Form reasons:** design, scale, massing, roof, materials, openings, and visual impact the refusal ties to the house itself.
  - Visual impact that names neither counts as both.
  - A refusal counts once in each category it cites. So the gate compares the share of counted refusals citing any site reason with the share citing any form reason.
- Do the Assignment.

**Decision gates.** Both are settled before Phase 1 code starts.
- **Refusal tally.** If fewer than half the counted refusals cite a site reason, the house's form matters more than the site. Then the order becomes Phase 0, Phase 2, Phase 3, Phase 1, Phase 4:
  - The rule pack (Phase 2) starts with only the rules that work on the house alone: plan depth, form, pitch, roof form, ridge height, openings, finishes. Siting rules wait for Phase 1.
  - The Exterior work (Phase 3) is built next.
  - Ranking works as in Phase 3, minus containment: layouts are sized by today's light rectangle `PlotLimits`.
  - The wedge becomes "the house's form and exterior checked against its council's guide".
  - Its success criterion becomes 10 self-builders designing a house whose form rules show no Fail.
- **Assignment.** Answers are recorded per file: the PDF drawings, the DXF and the owner brief.
  - The DXF is kept only if at least 3 of 5 would start from the DXF.
  - The PDF drawings are kept only if at least 3 of 5 would use them rather than redraw.
  - The owner brief is kept either way, since it is cheap.
  - If neither the PDF nor the DXF reaches 3 of 5, the hand-off positioning is reopened before any Phase 4 work.

**Phase 1: Site**
- **Model.** `site.ts` grows:
  - a boundary polygon, plus the road edge and the entrance point;
  - north, taken from the map;
  - a wastewater treatment area.

  The rectangle model remains for people with no site yet ("dream" mode).
- **Coordinates.** MapLibre gives the boundary in WGS84. All site geometry is computed and stored in Irish Transverse Mercator (EPSG:2157, metres), converted with proj4.
  - The house's placement is a transform from plan metres to ITM: an origin point plus a rotation.
  - The site plan and the DXF later use ITM coordinates in metres, so a technician can lay them over a survey or national mapping.
- **Buildable area and orientation.**
  - The edge or edges touching the road edge are the front. The edge most opposite is the rear. The rest are sides.
  - The front setback comes from the county rule pack when it gives one. Otherwise, and for the other setbacks, it uses `DEFAULT_SETBACKS`, which the user can edit.
  - The boundary inset by those setbacks is the buildable area.
  - The user sets the house's orientation (its main ridge direction). It defaults to parallel to the road.
- **The contract with the generator.**
  - The generator's `PlotLimits` stays a width and depth: the largest rectangle inside the buildable area, measured in the house's own frame at the chosen orientation.
  - After generation, each concept's real footprint polygon is checked for containment in the buildable area. Concepts that don't fit are dropped.
  - Each concept is placed centred on that largest rectangle, at the chosen orientation, and the containment check runs there. If it fails there, the generator tries other positions inside the buildable area before dropping the concept.
  - If every concept is dropped, the user sees a message naming what limits the fit (setbacks, the brief's size or the orientation), each with what can be changed: edit a setback, remove a room, or turn the house.
  - The user can then move and rotate the house. Siting rules re-evaluate on every move, every layout pick and every exterior change.
- **Accuracy.**
  - A boundary traced on satellite imagery is indicative and can be metres out. The user can type edge lengths instead.
  - Any distance check within 2 m of its threshold shows Check, never OK or Fail.
  - The site plan says "indicative, not for dimensioning".
  - Importing a surveyed boundary (DXF) is Phase 5.
- **Sightlines.** Entrance sightlines show "Measure on site", with the required distances from the cited standard shown for reference. The site model has no speed limit, road alignment or roadside hedges and walls to compute them from.
- **Terrain.** There is none in Phase 1. Rules that need levels (contours, skyline, ridge height against the land) show "Measure on site" until an elevation source is chosen (see Open Questions).
- **WebGL.** Only two contexts are allowed. On the Site step, the map takes the HouseViewer's place:
  - the viewer unmounts on Site and remounts when you leave it;
  - the house is drawn on the map as a MapLibre layer (footprint, and a fill-extrusion for massing);
  - so at most the map plus the shared stills renderer are alive at once.

  An ADR records this, because it reverses "the viewer stays mounted while hidden" for this one step.

**Phase 2: Rule packs as data**
- **Schema.** Each rule has:
  - an id;
  - the source's quote;
  - its source: document, edition or effective date, URL and page (county guide or national document);
  - what it measures (plan depth, ridge height, pitch, road setback, openings) and its thresholds;
  - its severity;
  - its stage: `layout` (footprint, plan depth, form, setbacks) or `exterior` (pitch, roof form, ridge height, finishes, windows).

  Rules are evaluated over the site plus the house geometry.
- **What the results mean.**
  - **OK** means "meets the measurable test on page N of [source, edition]". Each OK carries the line "Planners also judge things this app can't measure."
  - **Check** means near a threshold or a matter of judgement. **Fail** means the measurable test is not met. **Measure on site** means the app can't know.
  - There is never an overall "compliant" or "will get permission" verdict: not in the summary, and not in the pack.
  - This wording is fixed before any real self-builder sees Phase 2.
- **Exterior rules before Phase 3.** Roofs are fixed hips at 35° until Phase 3. Until then, exterior-stage rules show "Pending exterior", not a result.
- **Scope.** Every council from the start. The site's council comes from its location (council boundaries from Tailte Éireann's open data, CC BY, bundled and simplified), with an override. Before a council's pack is shown to self-builders, a technician agrees with its checks on 3 real designs from that council or a neighbour whose guide it closely follows.

**Phase 3: Exterior**
- Gable roofs (extending `roofModel.ts`, which builds hips from maximal rectangles today), a pitch choice, and main-plus-linked-wing forms using the existing shaped outlines.
- Wall finishes and window proportions on elevations.
- **Ranking.** The rule pack becomes an explicit tier inside `pickVaried`, which re-sorts candidates (prior learning: pickvaried-discards-presort). The order is:
  1. brief fit (the rooms asked for);
  2. layout-stage rules: any Fail ranks below every candidate without one, and Checks rank below clean candidates;
  3. plan sense, as today.

  Containment failures were already dropped in Phase 1. Exterior-stage rules don't rank layouts; they are evaluated at the Exterior step, against the user's choices.

**Phase 4: Hand off**
- **The pack.** Site 1:500, dimensioned plans 1:100, four elevations and a section as PDF; walls, doors and windows as DXF in ITM metres; an owner brief (rooms and why, what's fixed, what's flexible); the rule check with its sources. Every page says it is not planning drawings and has not been checked for building regulations.
- **New drafting work, each scoped on its own:**
  - automatic dimension chains on plans;
  - orthographic elevations from the building model (walls, openings, roof profile, ground line, wings in front of and behind each other);
  - one section.

  None of this exists today. If it runs long, the first pack is site plan, dimensioned plans, 3D views and the DXF. Elevations and the section follow.
- **"Send to my architect / technician" stores designs on a server for the first time.** A site location is effectively a home address, so this is personal data under GDPR. Before building it, these are fixed:
  - **Links:** signed, expiring links (30 days by default), no accounts; anyone with the link can view and comment.
  - **Retention:** the owner can revoke a link and delete the stored copy at any time, and the copy is deleted automatically when the link expires.
  - **Lawful basis:** the owner's consent, given when they create the link.
  - **Privacy notice:** says that the site location and brief are stored, where, and for how long.

**Phase 5: breadth.** More counties, UK design codes, IFC, then reusable house types for small builders (the Higharc direction).

**Paused while Phases 0–4 run:** new Drafted-parity items, furniture asset expansion, more Gemini render work.

### R2-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The generator contract sizes concepts to the largest rectangle inside the buildable area, but then places each concept centred in the buildable area and drops any concept whose footprint is not contained. On an irregular or non-convex boundary the largest rectangle is usually not at the centre (the centroid can even fall outside the polygon), so concepts that fit the rectangle can fail containment at the centred position and be dropped needlessly. The doc also does not say what the user sees if every concept is dropped.

**Remedy**

> Place each concept within the largest-rectangle position (e.g. centred on that rectangle) and run the containment check there, or search for a containing position before dropping a concept. Define the no-fit outcome: a message naming the constraint (setbacks, brief size, orientation) and what the user can change.

### R2-2 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The refusal-tally gate's alternative branch is not coherent with the phase dependencies. Phase 3's ranking uses the Phase 2 rule pack's layout-stage rules and says 'Containment failures were already dropped in Phase 1', and Phase 2 rules are 'evaluated over the site plus the house geometry'. If Phase 3 moves ahead of Phase 1, it is unclear whether Phase 2 also moves, what the rule pack evaluates against with only the light rectangle, what the ranking tier does without containment, and what the one-county wedge (defined as Phases 0-2) becomes.

**Remedy**

> Write out the reordered sequence for the 'form matters more' outcome: which phases run in what order, which rules (form, pitch, plan depth) work on the rectangle site, how the Phase 3 ranking works without Phase 1, and what the wedge and its success criteria become.

### R2-3 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> The refusal sample size is now undefined. Phase 0 dropped '20+', the Cross-Model section still proposes 50, and the new decision gate ('fewer than half the refusals') reorders the whole roadmap on a proportion whose sample has no minimum, so a handful of refusals could decide it. This persists R1-18 and now affects a decision.

**Remedy**

> Set the minimum number of refused applications (and the date range) the gate is computed on, say why that number is enough, and reconcile it with the Cross-Model section.

### R2-4 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> The gate cannot separate site from form as written. 'Visual impact' is cited in refusals for both siting and the house's design (scale, form, ridge height, materials), so counting it on the site side makes the gate close to always pass. The doc also does not say how a refusal that cites both a siting reason and a design reason is counted.

**Remedy**

> Define the tally's categories with a coding rule: which refusal reasons count as site (location, setback, access, wastewater, siting-related visual impact) and which as form (design, scale, roof, materials), how visual impact is split, and how multi-reason refusals count.

### R2-5 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The Assignment gate counts professionals who 'would start from the pack' and cuts the DXF on failure, but the Assignment itself says to 'count how many would start from the DXF', and the Success Criteria say 'the pack or DXF'. If 3 of 5 would start from the PDF but none from the DXF, the gate says Phase 4, DXF included, goes ahead as written; the measure and the consequence do not match.

**Remedy**

> Record the answers per file (PDF, DXF, owner brief) and tie each cut to its own count, e.g. keep the DXF only if 3 of 5 would start from the DXF. Use the same measure in the Assignment, the gate and the Success Criteria.

### R2-6 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The revision removed the Phase 2 line that classed sightlines as 'Measure on site' and now names road design standards as the source for entrance sightline thresholds, which implies the app computes sightline checks. Computing them needs road alignment, vertical geometry, speed limit and roadside obstructions (hedges, walls), none of which the Phase 1 site model has. Whether sightlines show a computed result or 'Measure on site' is now unspecified.

**Remedy**

> State that entrance sightlines show 'Measure on site' (with the cited required distances shown for reference) until the inputs exist, or add the inputs (speed limit, visibility splay drawn by the user) to Phase 1 and say how the result is classed given the 2 m Check band.

### R2-7 — feasibility (minor)

**Problem**

> Edge lengths alone do not define a polygon with four or more sides; the shape also needs angles or diagonals. The doc does not say how typed lengths combine with the traced boundary, or whether a typed boundary is still treated as indicative for the 2 m Check band.

**Remedy**

> Say that typed lengths adjust the traced edges while keeping their traced bearings (or require lengths plus a diagonal), and state that position on the map is still indicative.

### R2-8 — feasibility (minor)

**Problem**

> The same section says a traced boundary 'can be metres out', but the 2 m band is not tied to any measured accuracy of the chosen imagery. If the imagery is offset by more than 2 m, an OK can be wrong.

**Remedy**

> Base the band on the chosen tile provider's stated or measured positional accuracy in Ireland, or make it a per-source setting reviewed when the provider is chosen.

### R2-9 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The doc does not say whether picking a different layout after the user has moved or rotated the house keeps the user's position and orientation or re-centres the new concept, or what happens when the user drags the house outside the buildable area (blocked, or allowed with a Fail).

**Remedy**

> State the placement behaviour on layout pick and the behaviour when a move leaves the buildable area.

### R2-10 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

**Remedy**

### Responsive and accessibility

**13A. Phones.** On a phone the Map panel is the existing bottom sheet (`data-chrome="bottom"`).
- While tracing, the sheet collapses to a one-line task bar: "Tap each corner · 3 corners · Undo · Done".
- One-finger drag pans the map, a tap adds a corner, and pinch zooms.
- Corners are 44 px touch targets and drag to move.
- A corner can be removed with Undo, or with a long press and then Remove.
- The house's move and rotate grips (10A) are 44 px on `pointer: coarse`.

**14A. Keyboard and screen readers.**
- **Tracing by keyboard.** A crosshair sits at the map's centre. Arrow keys pan (Shift for larger steps), + and − zoom, Enter adds a corner at the crosshair, Backspace removes the last corner, and Escape leaves tracing.
- **Corners list.** The panel lists corners as "Corner 1 … n" with Remove buttons.
- **Live region.** A polite live region announces "Corner 3 added · area 0.31 ha" and states errors in words (3A).
- **Road edges and edge roles.** Each is chosen from a list of edges ("Edge 2, 41 m, north-east") as well as by tapping.
- **Other paths.** "No site yet" keeps the fully keyboard-usable plot form. The house's move and rotate (10A) have arrow-key equivalents once it is picked. The focus ring is DESIGN.md's single ring. Status is never shown by colour alone (7A).

### Remaining decisions

**15A. Find your site.** One field, with a visible label, "Eircode, address or townland", plus a **Use my location** button.
- Results appear as a short list. Picking one moves the map there; the user can also just drag the map.
- The search provider is named only in the privacy notice (11A, R3).
- No-result copy is as in 3A.

**16A. Motion.**
- The map moves to a found site over `--dur-slow`. That is navigation, like the plan's Fit glide, not show.
- The buildable area and house rectangle fade in over `--dur-base`.
- Nothing else animates, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` all durations are zero.
- The layouts reveal stays the product's one authored motion moment.
- "Doesn't meet" outlines are solid `--danger` with no glow.
